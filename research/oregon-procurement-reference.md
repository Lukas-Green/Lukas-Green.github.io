## 1. How Oregon public buying works

Public bodies cannot simply buy from whoever they like. Oregon's public
contracting code sets the process, and **the process gets heavier as the
dollar value rises.**

### 1.1 Goods and services thresholds

Governed by ORS Chapter 279B. Thresholds were raised by SB 1047, effective
2023-09-23.

| Band | Value | Method | What it takes to win |
|---|---|---|---|
| Small procurement | ≤ $25,000 | ORS 279B.065 | Agency discretion. Can be a direct award. |
| Intermediate | > $25,000 to ≤ $250,000 | ORS 279B.070 | At least three informally solicited quotes |
| Formal | > $250,000 | ORS 279B.055 / .060 | Public advertisement, sealed bids or full RFP, scoring committee |

**Why this matters more than anything else in this document.** The formal
band is where established firms compete and where a response costs dozens of
unpaid hours. The small-procurement band is where a firm without a track
record can actually be hired. A new firm's entire first year belongs under
$25,000.

### 1.2 Architecture, engineering and related services — a separate track

Governed by ORS 279C.100 through 279C.125. This covers architectural,
engineering, photogrammetric mapping, transportation planning, land
surveying, **and related services** — a category that pulls in interior
design and space planning.

The defining feature is **qualifications-based selection (QBS)** under
ORS 279C.110: an agency selects a consultant *on qualifications alone*.
Price cannot be considered until after the consultant is chosen. Pricing
policies, hourly rates, hours and overhead may be solicited only to set
compensation after selection.

**Read this correctly.** QBS is good news for a firm with strong credentials
and bad news for a firm with no track record, because "qualifications" in
practice means demonstrated past performance. QBS work is a year-two-or-later
target, not an entry point.

## 2. The certification landscape

### 2.1 What COBID is

The **Certification Office for Business Inclusion and Diversity**, an office
within Business Oregon (the state's economic development department).
Statutory authority: ORS 200.055, which names COBID as the sole agency that
may certify these business categories in Oregon.

COBID is not a program you join. It is an office that **verifies documents
and maintains an official list.** The list is the point: agencies search it,
and set-aside rules are written in terms of who is on it.

Certification is **free** — no application fee for any category.

### 2.2 The five Oregon categories

| Code | Full name | Basis | Verifies |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ESB** | Emerging Small Business | Size of business | Employee count and revenue below thresholds |
| **WBE** | Women Business Enterprise | The individual | 51% owned **and controlled** by a woman |
| **MBE** | Minority Business Enterprise | The individual | 51% owned **and controlled** by a minority owner |
| **VBE** | Veteran Business Enterprise | The individual | 51% owned by a veteran (see 2.5 — this changed) |
| **DBE** | Disadvantaged Business Enterprise | The individual | Individualized social + economic disadvantage (see 2.6 — this changed) |

There is **no Oregon category abbreviated "DSB."** If you have seen that
abbreviation, it is almost certainly one of DBE, VBE, or the older SDV
label. A separate private certification, **DOBE**, exists for
disability-owned businesses — see 2.7.

### 2.3 ESB — Emerging Small Business

**The category LUXIGA qualifies for today.** Race- and gender-neutral;
based purely on the size of the business.

Eligibility (OAR 123-200-1600):

- **Tier 1** — fewer than 19 employees, and three-year average gross annual
  receipts below **$1,972,900** (construction) or **$789,198** (non-construction)
- **Tier 2** — not more than 29 full-time-equivalent employees, with receipts
  below limits set by rule

Thresholds are **re-indexed annually to the Consumer Price Index**. The figures
above reflect the most recently published values found; confirm the current
year's numbers with COBID before filing.

- Certification valid **three years**
- Lifetime participation capped at **12 years**
- Business must be Oregon-based

### 2.4 WBE — Women Business Enterprise

Four tests, all of which must be met:

1. A woman owns at least **51%**
2. She **controls and manages daily operations**
3. She makes the **long-term decisions**
4. She holds any **professional licensing** the business requires

The applicant must sign the application herself and communicate directly with
COBID. Certification valid **two years**.

> **The control test is the one that gets enforced.** A firm where the
> certified owner holds title on paper while someone else runs the business is
> a "front" or "pass-through." Consequences documented in enforcement actions
> nationally: civil fraud liability, criminal false-statement charges, jail
> time, debarment from public contracting, and in some cases court-ordered
> dissolution. Several states have specifically tightened certification rules
> to detect this pattern.
>
> Legitimacy turns on a single question: **does the certified owner actually
> perform and direct the work?** Where the woman owner holds the professional
> credential the firm sells and makes the professional judgments, the structure
> is real. Where she does not, no operating agreement can make it real.

### 2.5 VBE — Veteran Business Enterprise (formerly SDV)

**This changed recently and widened substantially.** As of **2024-01-01**,
HB 2295 expanded certification from businesses owned by *service-disabled*
veterans to businesses owned by **any veteran**, and COBID renamed the
existing SDVBE certification to **Veteran Business Enterprise (VBE)**.

Requirements:

- 51% ownership and control by a veteran
- Documentation of **honorable discharge**, showing at least:
  - 90 days active duty before January 1955, or
  - 178 days active duty after January 1955, or
  - 1 day of combat zone deployment
- Three-year average gross annual receipts not exceeding **$31.84 million**
- U.S. citizen or lawfully admitted permanent resident

**Practical note:** the old name is still in wide circulation, so material
referring to "SDV" or "SDVBE" may be describing the pre-2024 rules. Anyone
who served and was honorably discharged should check current VBE eligibility
rather than assuming the service-disabled requirement still applies.

### 2.6 DBE — Disadvantaged Business Enterprise

Federal program administered in Oregon by COBID, for **federally-funded
transportation work** — projects funded by FHWA, FAA, or FTA. Relevant
locally to ODOT and TriMet contracting.

**This program changed fundamentally on 2025-10-03.** USDOT issued an interim
final rule (published 2025-09-30) that **eliminated race- and sex-based
presumptions of disadvantage.**

What that means in practice:

- Previously, women and members of certain minority groups were *presumed*
  socially and economically disadvantaged
- Now **every applicant** must provide an individualized **narrative and
  evidence** of social and economic disadvantage — specifically, evidence
  that relief is needed to redress the economic effects of actual prior
  discrimination
- Existing DBEs must **recertify** under the new standard; prior qualification
  does not carry forward
- Contracts executed before 2025-10-03 are not retroactively affected

Financial limits:

- **Personal net worth** may not exceed **$1.32 million**, excluding the
  applicant's primary residence and their interest in the business
- Three-year average gross annual receipts not exceeding **$31.84 million**
  (49 CFR 26.65)
- DBE certification does not expire, but is subject to recertification

**Who this opens the door to.** Because the presumptions were removed rather
than narrowed, DBE eligibility is now determined by individualized showing for
everyone. It is no longer a category defined by demographic membership. It is
also, correspondingly, a much heavier application — a documented personal
narrative with supporting evidence, not a checkbox. The program remains the
subject of active litigation and commentary, so confirm current standing
before investing effort.

### 2.7 DOBE — Disability-Owned Business Enterprise (not a state certification)

A **private** certification issued by **Disability:IN**, not by Oregon.

- At least 51% owned, operated, managed and controlled by a person with a
  disability
- Applied for through the Disability:IN Certification Hub
- Determinations typically around 30 days

Because it is not a state certification, **it does not trigger Oregon's
set-aside or direct-award rules.** Its value is in corporate supplier
diversity programs, where large companies track DOBE spend. Worth holding if
you sell to corporations; it does nothing for OregonBuys.

### 2.8 Application mechanics (all COBID categories)

- **Cost:** free
- **Where:** e-application through Business Oregon, or by mail/in person to
  COBID, 775 Summer St NE, Suite 200, Salem OR 97301
- **Processing:** COBID targets 60 days from a complete application; observed
  range **45–90 days** depending on volume
- **Incomplete applications are not processed** and restart the clock —
  assemble the full package before submitting
- Sitting down with all documents ready, the application itself takes roughly
  1.5 hours

Typical document package: business formation documents, proof of Oregon
registration, tax returns (three years, or since inception for a newer firm),
owner résumé, bank signature authority, lease or office documentation,
equipment list, description of services.

## 3. What certification actually unlocks

This is the mechanism that makes certification worth the paperwork.

| Contract value | Mechanism | Competition faced |
|---|---|---|
| ≤ $10,000 | State sheltered market direct award | None |
| ≤ $25,000 | Direct award of a small procurement to a COBID-certified firm | None |
| > $10,000 to ≤ $150,000 | Agency may use OregonBuys to invite COBID-certified firms into a solicitation | Invited firms only |
| ≤ $50,000 | Agency may limit competition entirely to COBID-certified businesses | Certified firms only |
| > $250,000 | No certification advantage in the procurement method itself | Open field |

Plus two indirect effects that matter as much as the rules:

1. **Directory visibility.** Certified firms appear in the state's searchable
   vendor directory. Procurement staff use it to find suppliers. An
   uncertified firm is invisible to that search.
2. **Prime contractor demand.** Large firms bidding public work often carry
   participation goals for certified subcontractors. That creates active
   demand for certified subs and is the normal route onto projects far larger
   than a small firm could win directly.

**Strategic read:** under $25,000, business development is not proposal
writing. It is being known by the people who hold direct-award authority.
That is a relationship problem.

## 4. Insurance and bonding

### 4.1 What is typically required

- **Commercial General Liability (CGL)** is required in most Oregon state
  agency contracts. DAS directs agencies to set limits by risk assessment,
  so requirements vary by contract.
- **Professional Liability / Errors & Omissions (E&O)** is commonly required
  alongside CGL for professional services. Typical minimum limits seen:
  **$1,000,000 per occurrence / $2,000,000 aggregate.**

### 4.2 When it must be in force

Insurance documentation is generally required **before the contract is
finalized** — that is, at execution, not at bid submission.

> **Cash-flow consequence:** a firm can respond to solicitations while
> carrying no coverage, and bind policies once an award is in hand. Obtain
> quotes in advance so binding is fast.
>
> **Caveat:** a minority of solicitations require a certificate of insurance
> *with the bid*. This is stated in the solicitation's insurance exhibit.
> Read that exhibit before investing response time. Some agencies will accept
> evidence of insurability (a broker's quote letter) in place of a bound
> policy — worth asking.

### 4.3 Market cost, small design firm

| Coverage | Typical monthly | Typical annual |
|---|---|---|
| General liability (graphic/web design) | ~$32 | ~$384 |
| General liability (Oregon small business average, $1M) | ~$42 | ~$504 |
| Professional liability / E&O (interior design) | ~$52 | ~$621 |

Representative E&O policy: $52/mo at $1M per occurrence / $1M aggregate with a
$1,000 deductible. Oregon general liability ranges roughly **$29–$388/mo**
across industries; design work sits at the low end because the risk profile is
low.

**Planning figure: ~$85/month for both, payable at first award.**

### 4.4 Bonding

Bid, performance and payment bonds attach to **public improvement** contracts
— construction — under ORS Chapter 279C, not to professional services
procured under 279B. A firm selling design, software and consulting services
does not encounter bonding. A firm moving into construction-adjacent work
does, and should treat it as a separate qualification problem.

## 6. Glossary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **ACDBE** | Airport Concessions DBE — DBE variant for airport concessions |
| **Capability statement** | One-page government-standard firm summary: competencies, differentiators, past performance, codes, certifications, contact. Not a portfolio. |
| **COBID** | Certification Office for Business Inclusion and Diversity |
| **Compliance matrix** | Table mapping every solicitation requirement to where the response addresses it |
| **DAS** | Oregon Department of Administrative Services — runs statewide procurement |
| **Direct award** | Contract awarded without competitive solicitation |
| **Front / pass-through** | A certified firm used to reach set-aside work that it does not actually perform or control. Fraud. |
| **ITB** | Invitation to Bid — award on price, specifications fixed |
| **NIGP code** | Commodity classification code. Determines which solicitation notices reach a registered vendor. |
| **OAR** | Oregon Administrative Rules |
| **ORS** | Oregon Revised Statutes |
| **Past performance** | Documented record of prior comparable contracts, with references |
| **Prime / sub** | Prime holds the contract; subcontractor works under the prime |
| **QBS** | Qualifications-Based Selection — selection on qualifications, price considered only after |
| **RFP** | Request for Proposals — award on evaluated criteria, not price alone |
| **RFQ** | Request for Qualifications — screens firms on capability, often precedes an RFP |
| **Responsiveness** | Whether a submission met every stated requirement. Non-responsive bids are rejected regardless of quality. |
| **Set-aside** | Contract reserved for a defined class of firms |
| **Sheltered market** | Oregon program reserving certain low-value contracts for certified firms |
| **UEI** | Unique Entity Identifier — federal entity ID, replaced DUNS |

## 8. Verify before relying

| Figure | Why it moves | Verify with |
|---|---|---|
| ESB receipts thresholds | Re-indexed annually to CPI | COBID |
| Procurement thresholds | Set by legislation (last changed by SB 1047, 2023) | DAS / current ORS |
| DBE personal net worth cap | Federal rule, indexed | COBID / 49 CFR 26.65 |
| DBE eligibility standard | Interim final rule, subject to litigation | COBID / USDOT |
| Insurance minimums | Set per contract by agency risk assessment | The solicitation's insurance exhibit |
| Insurance premiums | Market rates, vary by firm | Broker quote |

**Get the ownership structure of any multi-owner entity reviewed by an
attorney before forming it.** Certification categories based on ownership and
control carry legal exposure when the structure does not match reality.
