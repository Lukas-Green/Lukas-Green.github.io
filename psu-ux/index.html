Where pdx.edu loses the students it most needs to keep

The information is there. But a returning student who can't get a straight answer doesn't ask twice, and getting a straight answer is harder than it should be. This looks at where that happens, who it costs, and what a fixed version looks like.

Lukas Green · UX + QA · findings verified live on pdx.edu, July 2026

Independent student and design analysis. Not affiliated with, endorsed by, or produced for Portland State University.

The problem, in one line

A student who can't find the answer doesn't email for help. They drop.

  • Retention and enrollment are the priority right now, and findability sits directly upstream of both
  • The students most likely to get lost are the ones PSU most needs to keep: older, returning, second-career, and first-generation learners
  • This is a retention problem, not a tidiness one

Finding 1 · One task, two owners

The search works. It hands you two owners of the same task.

Search "major change" and pdx.edu returns the right pages (about 12,200 results, the Registrar's page first). The problem is what comes next: two official destinations for one task, and the rule that matters most is missing from the page where you actually act.

Finding 3 · Overloaded navigation

The tabs carry too much to read

Open "Student Life" and 46 links appear in one panel, across seven categories, so the issue is not missing structure, it is sheer volume. Fine for a 19-year-old with sharp eyes. A real scannability and low-vision cost for an older or returning learner, which is exactly the retention population.

Spotlight · The registration hub

Where all three converge, at the highest-stakes moment

Every finding above shows up at once in the place a student actually enrolls. This is the moment they decide to stay or go.

What I ran into, and hear from other students

  • Hard to use on a phone, which is where a working adult often is
  • No clear next step; the answer was often "look on the website"
  • A hold I only found when I tried to register
  • Requirements, deadlines, and holds scattered across places

This is not a marketing page. It's the transaction. If a returning student stalls here, the university doesn't just lose a session. It loses the student.

Owned by Registration: the office with the most direct stake in whether these students make it through.

Why this is retention, not tidiness

Fails a scanner? No.

None of these trip an automated accessibility check. The WCAG baseline holds.

Fails a person? Yes.

And it fails the exact students the university is measured on keeping.

The load lands on the students carrying the most already: working adults, older and returning learners, first-generation students, anyone without an adviser to call or the eyesight to skim a wall of small links. A scattered journey quietly costs the people with the least time and the least slack, and those are the people retention and enrollment depend on.
